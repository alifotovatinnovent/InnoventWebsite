01 / The imperative

A plant is instrumented. It is not necessarily visible.

Upstream, midstream and downstream sites already generate enormous volumes of data — SCADA historians, DCS tags, vibration probes, gas detectors, permit systems, inspection records. What is missing is not measurement. It is one place where all of it is correlated, judged and acted on while the process is still running.

01

SCADA fusion and alarm intelligence

SCADA tells you a value crossed a limit. It does not tell you that the same compressor tripped twice this week, that the standby unit is already in maintenance, or that a crew is working inside that zone right now. INNFINI ingests the historian alongside sensors, permits, work orders and personnel location, correlates them in real time, and presents the operator with a grouped, ranked event rather than a wall of individual alarms — each one carrying its own runbook.

Ingest
OPC UA, Modbus, CAN bus and Profibus straight from the control layer — no custom development
Correlate
The CEP rules engine evaluates every event against process state, asset history and who is on site
Respond
Runbooks attach to the alarm automatically and dispatch the field crew with the procedure already loaded
Record
Every recommendation cited, every approval logged, every action reversible
02

Condition monitoring and predictive maintenance

Critical and semi-critical rotating equipment is typically the only part of the plant with condition monitoring — yet it is just 15–25% of the fleet. Pumps, blowers, fin-fan coolers, agitators and the rest of the balance of plant run to failure, because conventional monitoring has been too expensive to justify per asset. Low-cost wireless beacons measuring vibration and temperature, with edge processing and cloud AI, extend predictive maintenance across the whole fleet.

Sense
A wireless beacon per asset measures vibration and skin temperature with intelligent edge processing
Learn
AI establishes each machine's own baseline, so deviation is judged against that asset rather than a generic threshold
Forecast
Failure probability and remaining useful life, with the work order raised before the trip
Act
Immediate notification when equipment stops or exceeds temperature limits
03

Gas detection, environmental and emissions monitoring

Fixed detection covers the points someone specified years ago. It does not cover the temporary works, the turnaround scaffold or the corner of the tank farm where the wind sits. A mesh of wireless sensors extends coverage where it is needed, at a cost that makes temporary deployment reasonable, and every reading — routine or otherwise — is retained as a defensible record of the conditions the plant operated under.

Measures
Combustible and toxic gas, temperature, humidity, air quality and CO₂, pressure and the rest of the process spectrum
Coverage
5,000+ sensors from leading providers already integrated; battery-powered units place in minutes, not shutdowns
Alerts
The rules engine fires the instant a reading breaches a limit — email, HTTP, pub/sub or web socket
Evidence
Every reading logged for audit, with historical playback of what happened, where and when
04

Lone-worker safety and personnel accountability

The question an incident commander asks first is how many people are inside the fence and where. On most sites the answer is a sign-in sheet and a radio check. Wearable tags and geo-fenced zones make it a live figure: who entered a confined space and when, who has been in a hot area past their permit window, who has stopped moving, and — at the muster point — who is still unaccounted for, by name, in seconds rather than in an hour.

Zones
Geo-fenced hazardous areas, confined spaces and restricted zones with entry, dwell and exit rules
Duress
Panic button, man-down detection and automatic check-in escalation across radio and SMS
Mustering
Live headcount by zone and by muster station, with an exception list rather than a roll call
Permits
Presence checked against the permit to work — the system knows if the wrong person is in the wrong place

05

Asset, tool and equipment tracking

Calibrated instruments, lifting gear, torque tools, spool pieces and rental equipment move constantly during a turnaround and are searched for constantly afterwards. Every item carries a tag and is read automatically as it moves — no line of sight, no manual scanning. The map shows the last read, the timestamp and the zone, and certification state travels with the item, so out-of-calibration gear is flagged before it is picked up rather than after it is used.

Reads
Patented long-range RFID — hundreds of items at once, no line of sight, passive-tag economics
Location
True RTLS using statistical radiometric methods across multiple receivers, not simple antenna association
Gates
Direction of travel and moving-vs-stationary discrimination at gates and laydown yards
Integration
Automated capture straight into the EAM or ERP — SAP included
06

Command and control tower

A field, a terminal and a refinery each run a dozen disconnected systems — process control, fire and gas, CCTV, access control, fleet, EAM, GIS — each with its own console and its own alarms. During an incident, operators swivel between screens and correlate by hand. The INNFINI control tower ingests from every source, correlates in real time and presents one operational picture across sites, with response playbooks automating the next step.

Layer 1
Data acquisition — technology-agnostic ingestion of SCADA, sensors, RFID, machine vision, GPS, BLE and third-party systems
Layer 2
Correlation and rules — the CEP engine and the visual INNFINI Flow composer
Layer 3
Operational picture — dashboards, map reports, playbooks and role-based sharing across sites and contractors
