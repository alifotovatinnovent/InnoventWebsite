Abu Dhabi Ports: 280,000 containers, one operating layer

SA
S. Al-Mahmoud · Innovent · Engineering

AD Ports Group runs one of the busiest port complexes in the Middle East. Containers move through six terminals. Cold-chain freight ages in real time. Suppliers, contractors, and last-mile carriers coordinate around the clock. None of that was easy when each system lived on its own.

The starting point

When we walked the site, AD Ports was running 14 separate systems for what should have been one workflow. The TOS didn't know what the yard hostlers were doing. The cold-chain loggers reported to a vendor portal. Supplier scheduling lived in email. Each gate had its own paper checklist.

What we changed

Innfini deployed as the enterprise digital-operations platform — reading from every existing system through pre-built connectors, writing back through the same fabric. The TOS still ran. The cold-chain hardware stayed. What changed is that they finally talked to each other.

  • Warehousing and logistics surfaced through one console.
  • Cold-chain temperature alerts arrived in seconds, not at delivery.
  • Supplier portals replaced email threads.
  • Last-mile fleet telemetry meant ETAs customers could plan against.

Result

Port-wide visibility. 24/7 cold-chain integrity. Streamlined supplier coordination. The operations director told us "we have one operating picture for the first time."

The lessons

Two things mattered. First: we didn't ask AD Ports to rip and replace anything. The existing systems work — they just weren't connected. Innfini's connector layer was the entire reason this deployment shipped in months instead of years.

Second: visibility isn't the same as automation. The first six months were about making operations visible. The next twelve are about making them automatic. Agentic dispatch, predictive cold-chain interventions, and automated supplier coordination are next.
