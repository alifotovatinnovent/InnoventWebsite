01 / The imperative

A store knows what it sold. It rarely knows what it has.

Stock on hand is a number in a system, reconciled by a count that happens a few times a year and is out of date the day it finishes. Everything downstream inherits that error: replenishment fires late, the website promises stock the shop floor cannot find, and the gap between the two is discovered at the till.

01

No item-level truth

Inventory is known at SKU level at best. Which items, in which store, in front or in the back, is a question nobody can answer between counts.

02

Counting costs selling

Manual counts take associates off the floor for days, and the accuracy they buy starts decaying the moment the count closes.

03

Out of stock, in stock

The item is in the building but not on the shelf. To the customer and to the system that is the same as not having it.

04

Disconnected stages

Factory, warehouse and store each tag, scan and record separately, so nothing follows the item end to end.

A full-store count stops being an event that closes the shop and becomes something an associate runs down an aisle. Counting item by item rather than SKU by SKU is what makes the rest possible: once you know which items are present, you also know which are missing, which are in the back, and which are on display in the wrong place.
